Morning Meeting

Morning Meeting Ideas for Pre-K and Kindergarten That Take 10 Minutes

By Cara ZelasNovember 20267 min read

← Back to all articles

You do not need 30 minutes to build meaningful classroom community. Ten focused, consistent minutes every morning will do more for your class culture than any single big event.

Morning meeting is one of the most research-supported practices in early childhood education. But it often gets dropped because teachers feel they do not have time for it, or because they tried it once and the class fell apart before the second activity.

The secret is to keep it smaller than you think you need to. Here is a 10-minute structure that actually works for Pre-K and Kindergarten, plus eight activities you can rotate through all year.

The 10-Minute Morning Meeting Structure

Keep the same three-part structure every day. Predictability is what makes it powerful.

  • Greeting (3 minutes): Every child is greeted by name. No child is skipped.
  • Share or Question of the Day (4 minutes): One simple question, brief answers, no monologues.
  • Activity (3 minutes): A game, song, or movement activity that connects the class before the day begins.

That is it. Three parts, ten minutes, done. The key is to run it at the same time, in the same spot, every morning without exception for the first month.

"The same greeting every morning tells children: this is a place where we know each other's names. That is the foundation of everything."

8 Morning Meeting Ideas for Pre-K and Kindergarten

3 min

1. The Wave and Name Greeting

Go around the circle. Each child waves at the child next to them and says their name: "Good morning, Lily." Lily waves back: "Good morning, Marcus." Quick, easy, and every child hears their own name spoken aloud.

4 min

2. Question of the Day with Two Choices

Ask a simple two-choice question: "Would you rather have a dog or a cat?" Children answer with a thumbs up or thumbs down, or move to one side of the room. No long explanations required. Great for shy children who do not yet feel safe speaking in front of the group.

3 min

3. Compliment Circle

Each child says one kind thing about the person next to them. If a child is stuck, give a prompt: "Tell us one thing you like about being in class with them." This takes practice but becomes magical by November.

3 min

4. Feelings Check-In with a Visual

Display a simple feelings chart with four or five emotions. Each child points to or names how they are feeling today. This takes under a minute per child and gives you invaluable information about who might need extra support before the day begins.

3 min

5. Mystery Greeting in Another Language

Each Monday, introduce how to say "good morning" in a new language. Spend the week greeting each other in that language. By Friday, every child knows a new word. By the end of the year, your class can greet each other in a dozen languages.

4 min

6. "I Spy a Friend Who..." Game

Say: "I spy a friend who is wearing something blue." All children wearing something blue stand up and wave. Rotate through different clues. Quick, active, requires no materials, and children learn to notice each other.

3 min

7. Gratitude Round

Each child says one thing they are grateful for. Keep answers brief. After a few weeks, children start to name each other: "I am grateful for my friend Zara because she helped me find my crayon." These moments are worth everything.

3 min

8. Three Deep Breaths Together

Close every morning meeting the same way: three slow, deep breaths together. Children put hands on their bellies, breathe in through their noses and out through their mouths. It takes 45 seconds and sets the entire class up for a calmer morning.

The One Thing That Makes Morning Meeting Fail

The most common reason morning meeting does not stick is inconsistency. Missing it on the busy days is exactly when you need it most. The days when you feel most pressed for time are the days a 10-minute connection ritual will pay the biggest dividends in class behavior and cooperation.

Put it in your schedule in pen, not pencil. Protect it like you protect literacy block.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should morning meeting be in Pre-K and Kindergarten?

For Pre-K and Kindergarten, 10 to 15 minutes is ideal. Young children have shorter attention spans, and a focused 10-minute meeting is far more effective than a 30-minute one that loses the group halfway through.

What should be included in a morning meeting for young children?

A simple morning meeting for Pre-K and Kindergarten includes three things: a greeting (everyone says each other's names), a brief share or question of the day, and one short activity or game. Keep it predictable so children know what to expect.

What are the benefits of morning meeting for Pre-K students?

Morning meeting helps children transition from home to school, builds a sense of belonging, develops oral language and listening skills, and sets a positive tone for the day. Children who start the day with a structured community ritual are more focused and cooperative throughout the morning.

What are some easy morning meeting greeting ideas for Kindergarten?

Great greeting ideas include a wave and name around the circle, a handshake greeting where children say each other's names, a greeting in a different language each week, or a simple song that includes each child's name. The key is that every child is greeted by name each morning.

Can morning meeting help with social skills in early childhood?

Yes. Morning meeting is one of the most effective vehicles for teaching social skills because it is a real, daily context for practice. Children practice listening, taking turns, using kind words, and responding to peers every single day. These repeated micro-practices build genuine social skills over time.
