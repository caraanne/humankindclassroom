7 Picture Books About Kindness for the Early Childhood Classroom

By Cara Zelas  |  November 2026  |  6 min read

A picture book about kindness does something that a lesson about kindness cannot: it puts children inside someone else's experience. When children watch a character choose kindness, miss an opportunity for kindness, or receive kindness they did not expect, they are building the neural pathways that make real-life empathy possible.

These seven books are ones I return to again and again in early childhood classrooms. Each one is followed by a discussion question and a simple classroom connection so the book becomes a teaching moment, not just a story.

1

Each Kindness

Jacqueline Woodson, illustrated by E.B. Lewis

This quietly devastating book tells the story of Chloe, who never accepts the new girl Maya's attempts at friendship. When Maya moves away, the teacher teaches a lesson about kindness using a stone dropped in water: each kind act ripples outward. Chloe realises too late that she missed her chance. It is one of the few children's books that does not resolve tidily, and that is exactly what makes it so powerful.

Try asking:

"Have you ever had a chance to be kind and missed it? How did it feel afterwards?"

2

Have You Filled a Bucket Today?

Carol McCloud, illustrated by David Messing

A classic for good reason. This book introduces the metaphor of an invisible bucket that holds our good feelings. Kind words and actions fill buckets; unkind ones dip from them. Children take to this metaphor instantly and use "bucket filling" language naturally in the classroom long after the read-aloud is over. It gives children a shared vocabulary for kindness that becomes part of your classroom culture.

Try asking:

"Can you think of something someone did today that filled your bucket? What did they do?"

3

The Invisible String

Patrice Karst, illustrated by Joanne Lew-Vriethoff

This book addresses connection, love, and the feeling that we are linked to the people who matter to us even when they are far away. While it is often used for separation anxiety, it is also a beautiful book about why kindness matters: because we are all connected. For early childhood classrooms, it opens rich conversations about community and belonging.

Try asking:

"Who are the people you feel connected to? What does that connection feel like?"

4

Last Stop on Market Street

Matt de la Pena, illustrated by Christian Robinson

CJ rides the bus with his grandmother every Sunday after church, and he notices everything they do not have. His grandmother gently reframes each observation to show him what they do have. The book is about gratitude and perspective, but it is also about seeing the beauty in others and the world around you, which is the root of kindness. The illustrations are spectacular and children return to them again and again.

Try asking:

"Can you think of something in our classroom that we might not notice, but that is actually really great?"

5

Big World of Little Dude

Cara Zelas

Little Dude is small but sees the world in a big way, noticing kindness, feelings, and connections that the grown-ups around him sometimes miss. This book was written specifically to introduce empathy and perspective-taking to the youngest readers, and it works beautifully as a read-aloud for Pre-K through Grade 2. The gentle humour makes it immediately engaging, while the warmth underneath opens real conversations about how we treat the people around us.

Try asking:

"What is something small that Little Dude noticed that made a big difference? Can you think of something small you notice at school?"

6

Wonder

R.J. Palacio (also available as a picture book adaptation)

The picture book adaptation of Wonder, titled We're All Wonders, retells Auggie's story for younger children. Auggie looks different from other children and just wants to be ordinary. The book makes a powerful case for kindness toward people who are different, and for the courage it takes to be kind when others are not. Even the youngest children connect with Auggie's desire to belong.

Try asking:

"Have you ever been kind to someone even when no one else was? What made you do it?"

7

The Dot

Peter H. Reynolds

Vashti believes she cannot draw. Her teacher encourages her to make just one mark and sign it. That small act of kindness changes Vashti's entire relationship with art and with herself. The book is about creative courage, but it is also a stunning illustration of how one moment of genuine encouragement can change a child's trajectory. For teachers, it is a reminder of the power they carry every single day.

Try asking:

"Can you think of a time when someone believed in you and it helped you try harder?"

"A picture book does not just tell children about kindness. It puts them inside the feeling of it."

How to Get the Most Out of a Kindness Read-Aloud

The read-aloud is the spark. What you do after is the lesson. After reading, give children time to talk to a partner before asking the whole group. Ask a question that connects the book to their own life. And then, wherever possible, follow up during the day: "Remember what Chloe missed in the story? I saw you make a different choice just now."

When kindness in books is connected to kindness in real life, children start to see the two as part of the same story.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best picture book about kindness for preschoolers?

Some of the most effective picture books about kindness for preschoolers include Each Kindness by Jacqueline Woodson, Have You Filled a Bucket Today? by Carol McCloud, and Big World of Little Dude by Cara Zelas. The best choice depends on what you are teaching.

How do you use a picture book to teach kindness?

The most effective way is to stop and wonder aloud during the read-aloud, ask a discussion question at the end, and then connect it to something children can do or practise. The book is the spark, not the lesson itself. The real teaching happens in the conversation and the action that follows.

Are there picture books about kindness for kindergarten?

Yes. Kindergarten is an ideal age for kindness picture books. Great choices include Each Kindness, Last Stop on Market Street, and We're All Wonders. Each offers a rich story and plenty of material for discussion about how our actions affect others.

How often should I read books about kindness in my classroom?

Weaving kindness-themed books into your regular read-aloud rotation consistently across the year is more effective than reading them all in one week. When children encounter kindness themes repeatedly in different stories, the messages become part of the classroom culture.

Can picture books about kindness help reduce conflict in the classroom?

Yes, over time. Picture books build empathy by giving children a window into someone else's perspective. Children who have been read stories featuring kindness, inclusion, and repair after conflict develop a richer vocabulary for navigating social situations themselves.
