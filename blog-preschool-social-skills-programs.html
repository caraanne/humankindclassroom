Best Social Skills Programs for Preschool and Pre-K: A Teacher's Comparison

By Cara Zelas humanKIND Classroom

If you are looking for a social skills program for your preschool or Pre-K classroom, you have real options. This guide walks through the most widely used programs -- what each one is, who it is designed for, and what makes each approach different -- so you can find the right fit for your classroom.

Teaching social skills proactively is one of the most impactful things an early childhood educator can do. Children who learn to name their feelings, make friends, solve problems and regulate their behavior in the early years carry those skills into every classroom and relationship they will have. The question is not whether to teach social skills -- it is how.

What to Look for in a Preschool Social Skills Program

Before comparing programs, it helps to know what actually works with young children. Research in early childhood development consistently points to a few key elements:

  • Story and narrative -- young children learn through story more than through instruction. Programs that use books, puppets or characters give children a way in.
  • Active practice -- children need to do, not just listen. Role play, movement, and hands-on activities embed skills in a way that passive lessons cannot.
  • Repetition across routines -- social skills develop through consistent, repeated practice, not a one-time lesson.
  • Low teacher prep -- the best program is the one teachers actually use. Overly complex curricula often get shelved.

The Top Preschool Social Skills Programs

Second Step Early Learning

Ages 3 to 5  |  28 weekly lessons

Second Step is one of the most widely adopted preschool social skills programs in the United States. It organizes learning into 28 weekly themes using puppets, Brain Builder games, and songs. Lessons teach listening, empathy, calming down, and joining play. It has a substantial research base and is often adopted at the district level for public preschool programs.

Best for: Schools with budget and time for a full structured curriculum. Requires some training and preparation.

Al's Pals: Kids Making Healthy Choices

Ages 3 to 6  |  Resiliency-based

Al's Pals is a resiliency-based curriculum that uses original songs, puppet scripts, and guided discussions. It focuses on helping preschoolers practice healthy decision-making, impulse control, and peaceful conflict resolution. Al's Pals is designed to be used in short daily sessions and is known for being engaging and child-centered.

Best for: Classrooms focused on impulse control and decision-making. Works well in Head Start and community preschool settings.

Preschool PATHS (Promoting Alternative Thinking Strategies)

Ages 3 to 5  |  Emotional literacy focus

PATHS focuses on building emotional literacy and preventing behavioral challenges. Teachers use puppets, storytelling, and a Compliment List routine to help children recognize feelings in themselves and others. PATHS has one of the strongest research bases of any preschool social-emotional program and is widely used in special education and inclusive settings.

Best for: Classrooms with children who need extra support with emotional regulation and behavior. Strong evidence base makes it a common choice for grant-funded programs.

The Incredible Years (Dina Dinosaur)

Ages 3 to 8  |  Small group coaching

The Incredible Years uses child-sized puppets and small-group activities to coach children on anger management, following rules, and making friends. It is especially designed for classrooms supporting children who need extra help with self-regulation. The program includes a parent component and is often used in clinical and therapeutic settings as well as classrooms.

Best for: Classrooms with children showing significant behavioral challenges. More intensive than most classroom programs.

humanKIND Classroom

Ages 3 to 8  |  Story-based  |  Free access available

humanKIND Classroom is a story-based social skills program for Pre-K through Grade 2, built around picture books and the five-step humanKIND Method: Spark, Story, Activate, Share, Reflect. The program covers nine social skills themes -- kindness, empathy, making friends, managing big feelings, self-regulation, courage, problem solving, inclusion, and gratitude -- using the Big World of Little Dude book series.

Each lesson uses a picture book to open a conversation, a hands-on activity to practice the skill, and a reflection discussion to close. Which Program Is Right for Your Classroom?

The right program depends on your classroom's specific needs, your school's budget, and how much preparation time you realistically have.

If your school has a district-wide social skills initiative and budget for professional development, Second Step or PATHS are strong choices with deep research backing.

If you are looking for something you can start using immediately without formal training, humanKIND Classroom is designed for exactly that. Lessons are built around books already familiar to early childhood classrooms, and the five-step method is simple enough to pick up in one reading.

If your class includes children with significant behavioral needs, The Incredible Years offers more intensive support than most classroom programs.

If budget is a constraint, humanKIND Classroom offers a free access program for qualifying Pre-K and Kindergarten classrooms.

Common Questions

What are the best social skills programs for preschool?

The most widely used preschool social skills programs include Second Step Early Learning, Al's Pals, Preschool PATHS, The Incredible Years, and humanKIND Classroom. Each uses a different approach. Second Step uses puppets and songs across 28 weekly themes. Al's Pals is resiliency-based for ages 3 to 6. PATHS focuses on emotional literacy. Incredible Years uses small group coaching. humanKIND Classroom uses picture books and the five-step humanKIND Method. The best fit depends on your classroom's needs, budget, and prep time.

How is humanKIND Classroom different from Second Step?

Second Step is a scripted 28-week curriculum using puppets, games and songs, with professional development required to implement it. humanKIND Classroom uses picture books and a five-step method across nine social skills themes, with no special training needed. humanKIND Classroom also offers free access for qualifying classrooms, while Second Step requires a school or district purchase.

What social skills does humanKIND Classroom teach?

humanKIND Classroom covers nine themes: kindness, empathy, making friends, managing big feelings, self-regulation, courage and trying new things, problem solving, inclusion, and gratitude. Each is taught through a picture book, a hands-on activity, and guided discussion.

What age group is humanKIND Classroom designed for?

humanKIND Classroom is designed for Pre-K through Grade 2, children ages 3 to 8. It is most used in preschool, pre-kindergarten and kindergarten classrooms, and extends naturally into first and second grade.
