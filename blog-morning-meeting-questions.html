Morning Meeting

50+ Morning Meeting Questions for Pre-K, Kindergarten and Grade 1

By Cara Zelas  |  November 2026  |  5 min read

A great morning meeting question does three things at once: it warms up the brain, it builds connection between children, and it creates a moment of genuine belonging before the academic day begins. But coming up with a fresh question every morning is harder than it sounds.

Here is a big bank of morning meeting questions for Pre-K, kindergarten, and Grade 1, organized by theme so you can pick one that fits what your class needs on any given day.

How to Use Morning Meeting Questions Well

Before the list, a few principles that make morning meeting questions work. First, pair-share before whole-group share. Have children turn and tell a friend their answer before you go around the circle. This gives every child a chance to practise and means shy children are not put on the spot in front of everyone.

Second, model your own answer first. When the teacher answers genuinely, children see that the question is safe to answer honestly. Third, celebrate interesting answers without ranking them. "Oh, I love that!" is better than "Great answer!" because it responds to the content rather than just approving of participation.

"The best morning meeting question is not the cleverest one. It is the one that makes every child feel like their answer matters."

Feelings and Emotions

  • How are you feeling this morning? Can you show me with your face?
  • What is something that made you smile recently?
  • What is something that felt hard for you this week?
  • Can you show me what your body feels like when you are excited?
  • What is something that makes you feel proud?
  • If your feelings were weather today, what would they be?
  • What is something that makes you feel safe?
  • What do you do when you feel frustrated?

Friendship and Kindness

  • What does a good friend do?
  • Can you tell us about a time someone was kind to you?
  • What is one kind thing you could do for someone today?
  • What makes someone a good friend to you specifically?
  • Have you ever helped someone who was sad? What did you do?
  • What would you do if you saw someone sitting alone at lunch?
  • What is the kindest thing anyone has ever done for you?
  • If you could give a friend a superpower, what would it be?

Would You Rather (Great for Pre-K)

  • Would you rather be able to fly or be invisible?
  • Would you rather have a pet dragon or a pet unicorn?
  • Would you rather eat only sweet things or only salty things forever?
  • Would you rather be a teacher or a scientist?
  • Would you rather live in a treehouse or underground?
  • Would you rather always be cold or always be hot?
  • Would you rather have ten puppies or one elephant?
  • Would you rather be tiny as an ant or giant as a dinosaur?

Favourites and Preferences

  • What is your favourite thing to do after school?
  • What is your favourite food and why?
  • What is your favourite animal?
  • What is the best book you have ever heard?
  • What is your favourite game to play with friends?
  • What is your favourite season and what do you love about it?
  • If you could visit anywhere in the world, where would you go?
  • What is your favourite thing about our classroom?

Imagination and Creativity

  • If you could invent something that does not exist yet, what would it be?
  • If animals could talk, which animal would have the most interesting things to say?
  • If you could live in any book, which one would you choose?
  • If you had one magic wish (not for more wishes!), what would you wish for?
  • If you were an animal for a day, what would you do?
  • What would your perfect day look like from start to finish?
  • If you had a shop, what would you sell?
  • What would you name a star if you discovered one?

Community and the World

  • What is one rule you think every classroom should have?
  • What is something you are good at that you could teach someone else?
  • What is something you want to learn this year?
  • How do you help the people in your family?
  • What is one thing you would change about the world if you could?
  • Who is someone in our school who is kind? What do they do?
  • What does it mean to take care of our planet?
  • What is something our class does really well together?

Getting to Know Each Other

  • What is something most people in this class do not know about you?
  • What is a tradition your family has that you love?
  • What language do you speak at home?
  • What is a food from your culture that you love?
  • What is something your family does together that makes you happy?
  • Who is someone in your life who you really look up to?
  • What is a nickname you have, or one you would like to have?
  • What is something you are hoping for this year?

Tips for Running the Questions Smoothly

Keep a running list of your class's favourite questions and repeat them across the year. Children love hearing that their answer is remembered ("Last month you said you wanted to be a vet. Is that still your dream?"). This kind of continuity is what makes morning meeting feel like community rather than a routine exercise.

For Pre-K specifically, visual supports help. You can display the question with a simple picture so non-readers can engage independently while you are still welcoming children into the circle.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good morning meeting question for preschoolers?

The best morning meeting questions for preschoolers are concrete, low-stakes, and either-or or open-ended. Good examples: "Would you rather fly or swim?" "What made you happy yesterday?" Questions that require children to share an opinion or preference build conversation skills without putting anyone on the spot.

How do you do morning meeting in a Pre-K classroom?

A typical Pre-K morning meeting runs 10 to 15 minutes and includes four parts: a greeting, a sharing question, a brief group activity, and a preview of the day. Keep it short, predictable, and joyful. The same structure every day builds anticipation and belonging.

How often should I change morning meeting questions?

Change your morning meeting question daily to keep children engaged. Some teachers draw from a jar of pre-written questions, others choose one each morning based on mood or theme. The question itself matters less than the routine of asking and listening together.

Can morning meeting questions help shy children?

Yes, with the right setup. Use pair-share first before asking the group. This gives shy children a chance to practise their answer before speaking in front of everyone. Never force a child to share if they are not ready.

What is the difference between a morning meeting question and a morning meeting activity?

A morning meeting question focuses on sharing and listening. A morning meeting activity is interactive and involves the whole group at once, such as a movement game or a quick collaborative challenge. Both are valuable, and a great morning meeting includes one of each.
